Drones dominate the air—and so do the systems built to stop them. Groundline is building the missing ground layer: low-cost autonomous rover swarms for persistent sensing, communications, and deep-field deployment.

Deep field

/dēp fēld/ noun

  1. The ground beyond the perimeter. Too remote to staff, too contested to risk people, and too long a watch for drones.

  2. Where Groundline operates. Persistent, autonomous presence on the ground, for hours or days at a time.

WHY GROUND

What drones can’t do.

Stay longer

Hours to days of useful deployment, because rovers don’t spend energy staying airborne.

Carry more

More battery, sensing, communications, and payload for the same size and cost class.

Harder to detect

Low profile, terrain masking, ground clutter, and no skyline silhouette.

Move quieter

Electric wheels and compliant tires carry a far lower acoustic signature than rotorcraft, especially at low speed.

Hold ground

Stop, observe, relay communications, or act as a sensor node with almost no propulsion power.

OPERATIONS

Hold the perimeter. Push into the deep field.

MISSIONS

From the wire to the deep field

01

Perimeter security

Persistent, coordinated patrol of bases, airfields, and critical sites. Units divide the perimeter, flag intrusions, and converge on contacts.

02

Remote monitoring

Rovers drive out, park, and become quiet observation posts, watching roads, valleys, and approaches for hours or days.

03

Communications relay

Units position themselves as a mobile mesh, extending and repairing links across terrain where line of sight is broken.

04

Deep-field sensor deployment

Carry and emplace sensors deep in the field without putting people in harm’s way, then keep reporting back through the swarm.

05

Combat power deployment

Move payloads and capability forward to where the fight is, without putting crews or vehicles at risk on the approach.
